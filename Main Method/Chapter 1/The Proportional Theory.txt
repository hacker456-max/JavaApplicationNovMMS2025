* The Proportional Theory: As you age, each year represents a smaller fraction of your total life. When you are 5, a single year is 20% of your existence, making it feel like an eternity. By the time you are 50, one year is just 2% of your life.
* Brain Processing Speed: In childhood, our brains are constantly wiring new neural pathways, absorbing vast amounts of new information, and processing high-definition experiences. As we mature, the brain develops mental shortcuts, processing familiar everyday routines much faster, which results in fewer lingering memories.
* The Antidote: You can trick your brain into "slowing down" time by continuously seeking out new experiences, traveling to unfamiliar places, and learning new skills. This forces your brain to pay closer attention and lay down rich, new memories.
